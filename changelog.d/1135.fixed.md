**`get_local_variables` and `evaluate_expression` say when the frame they answer from is not your program's.** On a stop where no frame is program code — a pause on an idle Node program, which lands in the timer loop; a `step_out` of `main` in C or C++, which lands in the C runtime — `get_stack_trace` and the pause and step answers said `noProgramFrame` and "No program frame is active…", while `get_local_variables` returned the runtime frame's variables (`now = 4210`, `[return value] = 0`) as plain locals, and `evaluate_expression` without a `frameId` ran there without a word. Both now carry `noProgramFrame: true` and the same sentence, with one clause of their own: "The variables shown belong to '<frame>', not to your program." and "The expression was evaluated in that frame." The variables are still returned — they are what the adapter reported — and an explicit `frameId` adds nothing, since the caller chose the frame. A Rust `step_out` of `main` is not covered yet: its landing frame is not classified as runtime (#1131). (#1135)

**`close_debug_session` on an attach session waits up to 10 s for the debug adapter to release the target, as `detach_from_process` does** — a target started with `python -m debugpy --listen` has one debug adapter, started once, which owns the port; it answers a `disconnect` only once the target's interpreter is free, and it exits when its client closes the connection before that answer is written. The close gave it 1 s and then closed the connection, so closing a session while the target held its interpreter for longer (a long C call, a backtracking regular expression) left the service running with its debug port gone (measured with 0.27.0: a close during a 4 s hold, the adapter process gone, connections refused). The close now sends the same release as `detach_from_process`, under the same 10 s bound, before the session's worker is stopped. The wait applies to the attach sessions of every engine: an adapter that does not answer keeps the close waiting up to 10 s where it was 1 s. A `close_debug_session` and a `detach_from_process` of one session no longer both send a confirmed release: one that arrives while the other waits for its answer waits for it, sends no second release once that release is confirmed, and answers from what it observed, also when that detach then fails because stopping its worker failed (a close sent 200 ms after a detach used to send a second `disconnect`, which ended debugpy's adapter and the target's debug port while both calls said `released: true`); a close that joins a detach whose release was not confirmed, whose request may never have reached the adapter, sends its own release under the same bound; the server's own exit does not join them. A `terminateProcess: true` detach during either is refused, and `start_debugging`, `restart_debugging` and `attach_to_process` are refused for as long as a close of their session is in flight, also once an `attach_to_process` that was in flight when the close came has answered. The close sends the release once the session is `running` or `paused`, which can be before `attach_to_process` has returned; that call can then answer before the close does, successfully or not. A close whose release was confirmed but whose stopping of the debugger failed says so in a `warning`, as does any other close whose stopping of the debugger failed; a child session's close then says neither `processEnded: true` nor `released: true`. When the adapter does not confirm it in time, for a close or a detach, the answer says so: the release was not confirmed within 10000 ms, the connection was closed (or, when stopping the debugger failed too, that whether it was closed is not known, and why), a debug port that belongs to the target may have closed with it, and whether the process is still running is not known. A target busy for longer than 10 s can still lose its port, and a tree's close is not bounded by 10 s (its children are closed first, each under a 1 s cap); the rest is tracked in #1325 (#1325)

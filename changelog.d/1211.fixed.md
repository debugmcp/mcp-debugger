**JavaScript: a forked child's breakpoints are verified from the child's own answers.** A fork adopted as a child session was read with the launch root's client behaviour, which mirrors breakpoints to an in-session target and trusts none of its own answers, so the adapter's ids never reached the child's breakpoint records. A breakpoint that bound on the line it was asked for was verified by its location; one that bound on another line (a comment line, the first line of a multi-line statement, a source-mapped line) stayed `verified: false` at the asked line in `list_breakpoints` after it had paused the child there, and a `set_breakpoint` on a child's id was treated as provisional. A child's records are now written from its own connection: the ids of the adoption's own answer, the bind event that follows (`line` moves to where it bound, `requestedLine` keeps what was asked), the stop that names it, and each later `set_breakpoint` on the child. The session layer reads a connection's client behaviour in one place, which gives a root its policy's and a child the child-safe derivative's (#1211).

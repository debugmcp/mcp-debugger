**JavaScript: a logpoint in a forked process is delivered to that process's session and no longer written into its stdout.** Only the program's own process had the debugger's channel for logpoint messages; in a fork the compiled logpoint fell back to the fork's `console.log`. With the fork's stdio piped, the text went down the pipe the parent reads: a parent that parses its child's stdout reported a corrupted protocol and exited with another code, and `get_output` showed the lines on no session. Every fork now gets the same per-process channel as the program (its own CDP socket, opened the moment the adapter can hand one out), and a logpoint hit before the channel is up is kept in the process and delivered in order, never printed and never paused for. The lines are on the session of the process that hit the logpoint: read them with `get_output` on the child's id. `get_output` on a session with child sessions now lists them in `children`, the list the launch and `wait_for_stop` answers carry, and a JavaScript child's note no longer claims that all of its output is on the launch session: what it writes to inherited stdio is there, what it writes to a pipe is read by its parent process only, and its own entries are its logpoint lines and notices. A fork's end is reported after its last lines (they were measured to arrive about 1 ms after the adapter's `terminated`): a fork that hits two logpoints and exits at once delivered both in 12 of 12 runs, and in none of 12 without that wait. A process that was sent a logpoint and whose channel cannot be opened says so in its output (`[mcp-debugger] Warning: logpoints that hit in this process are not delivered…`); for the program's own process that replaces the old silent fall-back to `console.log`. The program's own first statement gains too: a logpoint there used to go out through the program's stdout (measured on an ES module, 12 of 12 runs) and is now delivered like any other. What a process keeps while its channel is not up is bounded (1000 messages, 1,000,000 characters) and is not enumerable on its globals. **A process the debugger lets go of to run on is left clean**: a detach from an attached process, or closing a fork's session, used to leave the binding's function `__mcpDebuggerLogpoint` in the process (CDP's `Runtime.removeBinding` only ends the notifications); it is now taken out, with anything that was kept, before the adapter is told to disconnect (#1212).
